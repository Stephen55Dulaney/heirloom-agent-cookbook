# Technique: Ambition — planning the menu

> The first *decision* of the day, run after `/wake`. Wake tells you where you are; ambition
> decides where you're going. It's a short written plan — three to five named intentions for
> the day — produced in one of two modes, and knowing *which mode to use* is the technique.

## The move
1. **Gather the evidence** — yesterday's close-out (if you run [`ambition-close`](ambition-close.md)),
   the living memory's current priorities, anything on today's calendar, and any open loops the
   agent noticed overnight.
2. **Read yesterday's recommendation first.** If the previous close-out said "do X first," X goes
   first. The system is smarter than the habit; the bottleneck is reading and acting on your own
   system's output. (Field result: following one such recommendation flipped a day from 33%
   completion to 86%.)
3. **Pick the mode.**
   - **Automated** — the agent drafts the plan from the evidence and you approve it. Good for
     routine days. Consistent, fast, a little flat.
   - **Conversational** — you and the agent write it together, out loud, one intention at a
     time. Use this when energy is high and the day has variety. The dialogue surfaces context
     no generator finds, and it's where the joy lives.
4. **Name three to five ambitions** — each one concrete enough that tonight you could point at
   *evidence* it happened (a change made, a thing shipped, a conversation had). "Make progress
   on X" is not an ambition; "X does Y by end of day" is.
5. **Apply the carry-forward rule.** Any item carried from a previous day for **two weeks or
   more without being touched** gets a binary decision: **commit to it today, or drop it with a
   written reason.** There is no third option. Carried items are dead weight pretending to be
   intention.
6. **Write it down** — one dated file, same place every day, so the close-out has something to
   reconcile against tonight.

## Done when
A dated plan of 3–5 evidence-checkable ambitions exists, yesterday's recommendation was honored
or consciously overruled, and nothing on the list has been silently carried more than two weeks.

## Never
- Never let the agent's draft pass unread. The plan is yours; the agent is the sous-chef.
- Never write an ambition you couldn't verify with evidence at the end of the day.
- Never "carry again." Commit or drop — the third option is how backlogs rot.
