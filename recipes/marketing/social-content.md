# Recipe: The Town Square (Social Content)

> Short, shareable posts for the place where the crowd already gathers. You don't drag people to the
> square — you say something worth turning around for, and the crowd does the rest.

## What you're cooking

You know you should be "posting," but every post feels like shouting into the void. What do you even
say? How often? Which platform? And how do you do it without it eating your whole week?

This recipe builds a repeatable social practice: three to five content pillars so you never face a blank
page, hooks that stop the scroll, a system for turning one piece of work into many posts, a weekly
calendar, and an engagement routine that actually grows an audience. It covers LinkedIn, Twitter/X,
Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook.

Paste this card into your AI, tell it your goal, your audience, and your voice, and it helps you plan
pillars, draft posts, and set up a rhythm you can keep.

## Before you start

Read **The Mother Stock** first (`recipes/marketing/product-marketing-context.md`) if it exists — the
positioning, audience, and voice are there. Only ask for what it doesn't cover:

- **Goals** — brand awareness, leads, traffic, or community; the action you want people to take; and
  whether you're building a personal brand, a company brand, or both.
- **Audience** — who you're reaching, which platforms they live on, and what content they engage with.
- **Brand voice** — the tone (professional, casual, witty, authoritative), topics to avoid, and any
  style rules.
- **Resources** — how much time you can give weekly, what existing content you can repurpose, and
  whether you can make video.

## The method

**Know the platforms.** LinkedIn is for B2B and thought leadership, roughly 3-5 posts a week, carousels
and stories. Twitter/X is for tech, real-time, and community, many posts a day, threads and hot takes.
Instagram is for visual brands, one to two posts plus Stories daily, Reels and carousels. TikTok is for
awareness and younger audiences, one to four short videos a day. Facebook is for communities and local
businesses, native video and Groups.

**Build on pillars.** Pick three to five recurring themes that match your expertise and your audience,
and give each a rough share of the mix. For a SaaS founder that might be: industry insights (30%),
behind-the-scenes (25%), educational how-tos and frameworks (25%), personal stories and takes (15%), and
promotional (just 5%). For each pillar, ask what unique perspective you have, what your audience keeps
asking, what's worked before, what you can produce consistently, and what serves the business.

**The first line is everything** — it decides whether anyone reads the rest. Reach for a proven hook
shape. Curiosity: "I was wrong about [common belief]." Story: "3 years ago I [past state]. Today,
[current state]." Value: "How to [outcome] (without [pain]):" or "[Number] [things] that [outcome]:".
Contrarian: "Unpopular opinion: [bold statement]" or "[Common advice] is wrong. Here's why:".

**Repurpose so one thing becomes many.** Create a pillar piece (a blog post, video, or podcast), pull
three to five key insights out of it, adapt each to the platform's format and tone, spread them across
the week, and reshare the evergreen ones later. One blog post can become a LinkedIn insight, a LinkedIn
carousel, a Twitter thread, an Instagram carousel, and a Reel.

**Work in batches, publish on a calendar.** Spend two to three hours weekly reviewing pillar topics and
writing the week's posts — say five LinkedIn posts, three Twitter threads plus daily tweets, and a
couple of Instagram ideas — then schedule the evergreen core content and *leave gaps* for real-time
posts. Schedule the planned pieces; post live for commentary on news and for engaging with others.

**Engagement is half the job.** Spend about 30 minutes a day: reply to every comment on your posts,
comment thoughtfully on 5-10 posts from target accounts, reshare with your own added insight, and send a
couple of genuine DMs. Quality comments add a new idea, a related experience, or a real question — never
"Great post!" Pick 20-50 accounts in your space and show up in their comments consistently; that's how
relationships and, eventually, collaborations start.

**Review weekly and adjust.** Watch the metrics that matter: for awareness, impressions, reach, and
follower growth; for engagement, engagement rate, comments (worth more than likes), shares, and saves;
for conversion, link clicks, profile visits, DMs, and attributed leads. Each week, study your top three
and bottom three posts and ask why. If engagement is low, test new hooks, times, and formats. If reach is
falling, keep external links out of the post body, engage more in comments, and lean into video.

When you want to get systematic, reverse-engineer what already works: find 10-20 high-engagement
creators in your niche, study their hooks, formats, and CTAs, codify the repeatable patterns, then layer
your own authentic voice on top.

## Acceptance Criteria (how you know it worked)

- [ ] Three to five pillars are defined, each with a rough share of the content mix and a reason it fits.
- [ ] Every drafted post opens with a real hook, not a warm-up line.
- [ ] There's a plan to turn at least one pillar piece into several platform-specific posts.
- [ ] A weekly calendar and a batching routine exist, with gaps left for real-time posts.
- [ ] A daily engagement routine is defined, with quality-comment guidance beyond "Great post!"
- [ ] The metrics to review each week are named and tied to the stated goal.

## Primary metric

Engagement rate on published posts — comments, shares, and saves relative to reach — the honest signal
that the crowd found the post worth reacting to.

## The bright lines (never cross)

- **Never post to a pillar you can't sustain.** Consistency beats a burst that burns out.
- **Never fake engagement** with hollow "Great post!" comments or manufactured numbers.
- **Never fabricate the data, results, or stories** a post claims — especially in thought-leadership
  posts.
- **Never let promotional content crowd the mix.** Keep it near that 5% — the square empties when every
  post is a pitch.

---

*The square works because it's a crowd of people, not an audience of targets. Say the true, useful,
human thing — consistently — and the gathering grows around it.*
