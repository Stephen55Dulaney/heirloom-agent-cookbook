# Recipe: The Slow Drip (Email Sequence)

> A timed series of messages that steep over days — each one small, each one earning the next. Not a
> flood. A drip, patient enough that trust has time to build.

## What you're cooking

Someone signed up, downloaded your guide, started a trial, or went quiet — and now there's a moment to
build a relationship instead of letting them drift. A single email won't do it. What you need is a
sequence: a handful of messages, spaced over days, that lead someone from "just arrived" to "ready to
act" without ever feeling pushy.

This recipe designs that sequence: the right number of emails, the right gaps between them, a subject
line and preview for each, the body copy, and one clear next step per message. It works for welcome
and onboarding flows, lead nurture, re-engagement of people who've gone cold, and post-purchase
follow-up.

Paste this card into your AI, tell it what triggers the sequence and what you want it to achieve, and
it drafts the whole flow — every email, in order, with the timing.

## Before you start

Read **The Mother Stock** first (`recipes/marketing/product-marketing-context.md`) if it exists — the
positioning, voice, and proof points live there. Only ask for what it doesn't cover:

- **Sequence type** — welcome/onboarding, lead nurture, re-engagement, post-purchase, event-based,
  educational, or sales.
- **Audience context** — who they are, what triggered them into the sequence, what they already
  believe, and their current relationship with you.
- **Goal** — the primary conversion action, plus any relationship or segmentation goals, and what
  counts as success.
- **The surrounding traffic** — what other emails they're already getting, so this doesn't duplicate or
  pile on.

## The method

Four principles run underneath everything. **One email, one job** — a single purpose and a single
primary call-to-action per message; don't try to do everything at once. **Value before ask** — lead
with usefulness and earn the right to sell. **Relevance over volume** — fewer, better, well-segmented
emails beat frequency. **A clear path forward** — every email moves the reader somewhere, and the next
step is obvious.

**Length and timing depend on the type.** Welcome: 5-7 emails over roughly 12-14 days. Lead nurture:
6-8 emails over two to three weeks. Onboarding: 5-7 emails over 14 days (and it *supports* in-app
onboarding rather than duplicating it). Re-engagement: 3-4 emails over about two weeks, triggered by
30-60 days of inactivity. Space them tighter early (the welcome goes out immediately, the next ones a
day or two apart) and wider later (nurture two to four days apart, long-term weekly or bi-weekly). For
B2B, avoid weekends; send at the reader's local time.

**Each type has a proven arc.** A welcome sequence runs: welcome and deliver the promised value → a
quick win → the story of why you exist → social proof → overcome the main objection → highlight the core
feature → the conversion ask. Lead nurture: deliver the lead magnet → expand the topic → deep-dive the
problem → offer a solution framework → a case study → differentiation → objection handler → the direct
offer. Re-engagement: a genuine check-in → a reminder of what's new → an incentive → a last-chance "stay
or say goodbye." Onboarding: welcome and first step → getting-started help → a feature highlight → a
success story → a check-in → an advanced tip → the upgrade nudge.

**Subject lines:** clear beats clever, specific beats vague, 40-60 characters, benefit- or curiosity-
driven. Patterns that work — a question ("Still struggling with X?"), a how-to ("How to [outcome] in
[timeframe]"), a number ("3 ways to [benefit]"), the direct ("[Name], your [thing] is ready"), or a
story tease ("The mistake I made with [topic]"). **Preview text** (about 90-140 characters) extends the
subject rather than repeating it — finish the thought or add intrigue.

**Write each email in five beats:** a hook (the first line grabs), context (why it matters to them), the
value (the useful part), one CTA (what to do next), and a warm human sign-off. Keep paragraphs to one to
three sentences with white space between them; it will be read on a phone. Stay conversational and
active — read it aloud and ask whether a human would say it that way. Length follows purpose: 50-125
words for transactional, 150-300 for educational, 300-500 for story-driven. One primary CTA per email,
its button text pairing an action with the outcome.

Deliver the sequence with an overview up top (name, trigger, goal, length, timing, and the exit
conditions that pull someone out early), then each email laid out with its timing, subject, preview,
full body, and CTA. Close with a short plan for what to measure.

## Acceptance Criteria (how you know it worked)

- [ ] The sequence length and spacing match its type (a welcome flow isn't ten emails in three days).
- [ ] Every email has exactly one primary call-to-action.
- [ ] The early emails deliver value before any hard ask appears.
- [ ] Each subject line is clear, roughly 40-60 characters, with preview text that extends rather than
  repeats it.
- [ ] Exit conditions are defined — a reader who converts or unsubscribes stops receiving the rest.
- [ ] Read aloud, every email sounds like a person, not a broadcast.

## Primary metric

Sequence completion-to-conversion rate — the share of people who enter the sequence and take its primary
action by the end.

## The bright lines (never cross)

- **Never stack multiple asks into one email.** One job, one CTA — always.
- **Never open with the ask before you've delivered value.** Earn it first.
- **Never fabricate the case studies, numbers, or testimonials** a nurture email leans on.
- **Never omit the unsubscribe or the graceful exit.** A re-engagement sequence that won't let go is
  spam, not care.

---

*The slowest part of the drip is the point of it. Give it time, keep each drop small and honest, and by
the last one you've built something a single loud message never could.*
