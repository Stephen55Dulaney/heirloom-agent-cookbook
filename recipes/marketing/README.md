# The Market Stall — the marketing recipes

> An heirloom is kept by giving it away. Marketing is the giving-away made craft: carrying the
> harvest out to the crowd, hand to hand, and telling its story honestly enough that people reach
> for it.

This wing of the cookbook holds **31 recipe cards for marketing a real product** — distilled from
skills battle-tested on a live marketing benchmark, where frontier models cooked from these exact
instructions and were scored on what came out.
Like every card in this cookbook, they contain **no code**. Each is a plain-language contract:
what the work must do, what the AI must ask you first, the method that makes it good, how you
know it worked, and the lines it must never cross. Paste a card into Claude Code, Codex, or any
capable agent and say *"do this for my product."*

## Start with the Mother Stock

Every dish in this stall is built on one base: **[The Mother Stock](product-marketing-context.md)**
— your product's positioning, audience, voice, and proof, gathered once into a single document.
Cook it first. Every other card reads it before asking you a single question, the way a kitchen
draws from the stockpot instead of starting each sauce from scratch.

## How to cook from this stall

1. **Make the Mother Stock** — hand `product-marketing-context.md` to your AI. It interviews you
   once; every later card reads the result instead of re-asking.
2. **Pick a dish** — the card for the job in front of you today.
3. **Hand it over** — *"Read this card and do it for my product. The acceptance criteria are your
   tests. Honor the bright lines."*
4. **Check the criteria** — every card ends with checkboxes and one primary metric. Don't accept
   "done"; accept the boxes ticked.

## About the primary metrics (they are not Hall of Fame numbers)

This cookbook uses the phrase "primary metric" in two different ways, and the difference matters.

A card in `contracts/` names a number the **maintainer reproduces** on reference-class hardware to
award a belt in `hall-of-fame/` — time to first word, recall after reboot, minutes to a verified pin
map. Same board, same conditions, one champion.

A card in this stall names a number **you measure on your own product** — a conversion rate, a reply
rate, a signup rate. It depends entirely on your audience, your offer, and your market. Nobody can
reproduce it, so there is no belt for it and no leaderboard here. It exists so that "done" means
*something moved*, not *the AI produced words*. Measure it before you change anything, change one
thing, and measure again.

## The floor still holds

The Care Doctrine (`doctrine/care-doctrine.md`) is this cookbook's moral floor, and it doesn't
stop at the stall. Marketing built from these cards persuades by being *true*: no invented
testimonials, no fabricated numbers, no dark patterns, no weaponized psychology. A campaign that
converts brilliantly by misleading people is a failed build here, same as a companion that lies.
