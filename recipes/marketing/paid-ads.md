# Recipe: Renting the Corner (Paid Ads)

> The busiest corner in the market isn't yours, and you can't build it. But you can rent a spot on it —
> for exactly as long as it pays for itself, and not a day longer.

## What you're cooking

You have something to sell and not enough people finding it. Word of mouth is slow, search takes months to
climb, and you need customers now. Paid advertising is renting a place where the crowd already gathers —
Google when people are searching for what you offer, Meta (Facebook and Instagram) when you want to create
demand with a strong image or video, LinkedIn when you're selling to businesses and the job title matters.

This recipe turns an AI into a performance marketer — someone whose whole trade is buying attention for less
than it returns. It produces a campaign plan built for your goal: which platform fits, how to structure the
account, what the ads should say, who to target, and — the part most people skip — how to tell whether it's
working before you've burned the budget finding out.

You don't need to run the platforms yourself to use this. Paste this card into Claude Code or Codex, say "do
this for my product," and it will draw up the plan and the ad copy with you, explaining each call so you can
push back before a dollar is spent.

## Before you start

Tell the AI to read **The Mother Stock** first — `recipes/marketing/product-marketing-context.md`, the
master positioning document — if it exists. That's where your audience, your offer, your proof, and your
voice already live; the AI should draw on it and only ask you for what's missing.

Then it needs the numbers that make an ad plan honest: the **goal** (awareness, traffic, leads, or sales)
and the target cost per acquisition (CPA — what you're willing to pay for one customer) or return on ad
spend (ROAS — how many dollars back per dollar in); the **budget** per week or month; the **offer** and the
exact landing page the ad points to; the **audience** and the problem you solve for them; and the **current
state** — have you run ads before, what worked, and is conversion tracking already in place. That last one is
non-negotiable: an ad campaign with no tracking is money poured into a room with the lights off.

## The method

**Pick the corner before you rent it.** Google Ads for high-intent search — people actively looking for your
solution. Meta for demand generation and visual products where strong creative carries the pitch. LinkedIn
for B2B and decision-makers, where targeting by job title, company, or industry earns its higher price.
Twitter/X for tech and timely content; TikTok for a younger audience (skewing 18–34) and video you can make.
Start on one platform, not five.

**Structure the account so you can read it.** The shape is Account → Campaign (one objective, one
audience or product) → Ad Set (one targeting variation) → Ads (always two or three creative variations, never
just one). Name everything to a convention so a stranger can read the account: platform, objective, audience,
offer, date — for example "META, Conversions, Lookalike-Customers, Free-Trial, 2026-Q1." Consistent names are
how you find the winners later.

**Split the budget between safe and searching.** In the first two to four weeks, put about 70% behind proven
or safe campaigns and 30% into testing new audiences and creative. Once winners emerge, consolidate into them
and scale *slowly* — raise budgets 20–30% at a time and wait three to five days between increases so the
platform's algorithm can relearn. Big sudden changes reset the learning and waste spend.

**Write ads to a frame, not from a blank page.** Three reliable ones: Problem-Agitate-Solve (name the
problem, press on the pain, introduce the fix, ask for the click); Before-After-Bridge (the painful now, the
better after, your product as the bridge); or Social-Proof-Lead (open with a real stat or testimonial, then
what you do, then the ask). For video, the first three seconds decide whether anyone watches — hook, then
problem, then solution, then a clear next step, all in 15–30 seconds. Caption everything; 85% watch without
sound. Test the biggest lever first: concept and angle beat hook, which beats visual style, which beats body
copy, which beats the button wording.

**Target by each platform's real strength.** Google reads search intent (the words they type). Meta reads
interests and behavior, and can build "lookalikes" — new people who resemble your customers; base those on
your *best* customers by lifetime value, not all of them. LinkedIn reads professional identity. Retarget by
funnel stage rather than lumping everyone together: hot audiences (cart or trial, a 1–7 day window) get
urgency; warm (visited key pages, 7–30 days) get case studies; cold (any visit, 30–90 days) get education.
Always exclude existing customers and recent converters so you're not paying to reach people you already won.

**Optimize by the symptom.** If CPA is too high, check the landing page first — the problem is often after
the click — then tighten targeting, test new angles, improve ad relevance. If click-through is low, the
creative isn't resonating or the audience is wrong or the ad has gone stale (refresh it). If CPM (cost per
thousand impressions) is high, the audience may be too narrow or the competition too fierce. On bidding,
start manual or with cost caps, gather at least 50 conversions, then move to automated targets built on that
real history.

**Review weekly, and trust the blended number.** Each week check spend pacing, CPA/ROAS against target, top
and bottom ads, audience breakdown, frequency (fatigue risk), and landing-page conversion. Platforms
over-report their own results, so keep UTM parameters consistent, compare platform data against your own
analytics, and judge by blended customer acquisition cost — total spend over total customers — not the rosy
number the platform shows you.

## Acceptance Criteria (how you know it worked)

- [ ] Conversion tracking is live and tested with a real conversion *before* any campaign launches.
- [ ] The plan names one starting platform and gives a reason tied to the goal and audience.
- [ ] The account has a naming convention, and every ad set holds at least two or three creative variations.
- [ ] There is a clear target CPA or ROAS, and a stated budget split between proven and testing spend.
- [ ] Existing customers and recent converters are excluded from prospecting audiences.
- [ ] Ad copy traces to a named framework and every claim, stat, and testimonial is real.

## Primary metric

Cost per acquisition against target — what you pay for one customer, measured blended across the whole
funnel, held to the number you set before launch. If it beats target, the corner is worth renting.

## The bright lines (never cross)

- **Never launch without conversion tracking.** Ads with no measurement is guessing with a credit card.
- **Never make big sudden budget changes.** Move 20–30% at a time; a jump resets the algorithm's learning.
- **Never run one ad per ad set.** Without variations you learn nothing and fatigue kills the single creative.
- **Never fabricate proof in an ad.** Every statistic and testimonial must be real and attributable.
- **Never trust platform attribution alone.** Compare against your own analytics and judge on blended CAC.

---

*You don't own the corner and you never will — that's fine. Rent it clear-eyed, measure what it returns, and
keep the spot only as long as the crowd it brings pays more than the rent.*
