# Recipe: The Idea Pantry (Marketing Ideas)

> A stocked shelf of growth moves to reach for when the well runs dry — 139 proven tactics, sorted,
> so you pull the two or three that fit tonight's dish, not all of them at once.

## What you're cooking

Sometimes you don't need a strategy, you need an idea — a specific, proven move to try this week. **This
recipe is a well-stocked pantry of 139 marketing tactics, organized by category, stage, budget, and
timeline, so that when you're stuck you can pull down the few that actually fit your situation** rather
than staring at a blank shelf.

The pain it solves is the paralysis of "I know I should be marketing, but I don't know what to *do*." The
recipe's real value isn't the list — it's the matching: the AI reads your context and hands you 3-5 ideas
that fit your stage and resources, each with concrete first steps, not 139 options to drown in.

Paste this card into Claude Code or Codex and say "give me marketing ideas." The AI acts as a strategist
with the full library and recommends the ones that fit you.

## Before you start

**Read The Mother Stock first** (`recipes/marketing/product-marketing-context.md` — the master
positioning document). It holds your audience, stage, and differentiation — the context that decides which
ideas fit. Only ask for what it doesn't cover: your current stage and main growth goal, your budget and
team size, what you've already tried that worked or didn't, and which competitor tactics you admire. If no
Mother Stock exists, offer to build it first.

## The method

**Recommend, don't dump.** When asked for ideas, the AI's job is to ask about product, audience, and stage
(if not already clear from The Mother Stock), then suggest **the 3-5 most relevant ideas** and go deep on
those — always weighing the person's real resources of time, budget, and team size. A wall of 139 ideas
helps no one.

**The pantry, by category (139 ideas in fifteen shelves):**

- **Content & SEO (1-10)** — easy keyword ranking, glossary marketing, programmatic SEO, content
  repurposing, proprietary-data content, content refreshing, parasite SEO.
- **Competitor & comparison (11-13)** — comparison pages, "marketing jiu-jitsu" (turn a competitor's
  weakness into your campaign), competitive ad research.
- **Free tools & engineering (14-22)** — calculators, generators, scanners, quizzes, importers, Chrome
  extensions, microsites, public APIs — useful utilities that lead to the paid product.
- **Paid advertising (23-34)** — LinkedIn, Google, retargeting, podcast ads.
- **Social & community (35-44)** — LinkedIn audience-building, Reddit marketing, short-form video, comment
  marketing.
- **Email (45-53)** — founder emails, onboarding sequences, win-back campaigns.
- **Partnerships & programs (54-64)** — affiliate programs, integration marketing, newsletter swaps,
  expert networks.
- **Events & speaking (65-72)** — webinars, conference speaking, virtual summits, sponsorship.
- **PR & media (73-76)** — press coverage, documentaries.
- **Launches & promotions (77-86)** — Product Hunt, waitlist referrals, early-access pricing, lifetime
  deals, giveaways.
- **Product-led growth (87-96)** — viral loops, powered-by marketing, free migrations, in-app upsells.
- **Content formats (97-109)** — podcasts, courses, books, annual reports, year-in-review wraps.
- **Unconventional & creative (110-122)** — awards, challenges, guerrilla marketing.
- **Platforms & marketplaces (123-130)** — app marketplaces, review sites, YouTube.
- **International (131-132), developer (133-136), audience-specific (137-139)** — localization, DevRel and
  certifications, referrals and customer-language mining.

**Match to the moment, not just the category.** The same pantry sorts three other ways:

- **By stage.** *Pre-launch:* waitlist referrals, early-access pricing, Product Hunt prep. *Early:* content
  & SEO, community, founder-led sales. *Growth:* paid acquisition, partnerships, events. *Scale:* brand
  campaigns, international, media acquisitions.
- **By budget.** *Free:* content & SEO, community, social, comment marketing. *Low:* targeted ads,
  sponsorships, free tools. *Medium:* events, partnerships, PR. *High:* acquisitions, conferences, brand.
- **By timeline.** *Quick wins:* ads, email, social posts. *Medium-term:* content, SEO, community.
  *Long-term:* brand, thought leadership, platform effects.

**And by what you need right now.** Need leads fast → Google Ads (high intent), LinkedIn Ads (B2B),
engineering-as-marketing (free-tool lead gen). Building authority → conference speaking, a book, podcasts.
Low-budget growth → easy keyword ranking, Reddit, comment marketing. Product-led growth → viral loops,
powered-by marketing, in-app upsells. Enterprise sales → investor marketing, expert networks, conference
sponsorship.

**Deliver each recommended idea the same way**, so the person can act: the idea in one line, *why it fits*
their specific situation, *how to start* (the first 2-3 steps), the *expected outcome* (what success looks
like), and the *resources needed* (time, budget, skills).

## Acceptance Criteria (how you know it worked)

- [ ] **3-5 ideas, not 139** — the AI narrowed to a shortlist matched to this product, rather than pasting the whole library.
- [ ] **Each idea says why it fits** this person's stage, budget, and audience — not a generic pitch.
- [ ] **Each idea has 2-3 concrete first steps** someone could start on today.
- [ ] **Resources are stated** — the time, budget, and skills each idea needs, so nothing recommended is out of reach.
- [ ] **The match respects constraints** — no "high budget" tactics handed to a solo founder with no ad spend.
- [ ] **Expected outcome is named** for each, so success is checkable later.

## Primary metric

`fit` — of the ideas recommended, how many the person judges genuinely doable and worth trying given their
real stage and resources. A pantry is only useful if what you pull off the shelf can actually go in the pot.

## The bright lines (never cross)

- **Never dump the whole list.** The value is the curation; handing over 139 undifferentiated ideas is a
  failure, not thoroughness.
- **Never recommend beyond the person's means.** Match ideas to actual budget, team, and time — a tactic
  they can't afford to run isn't an idea, it's a tease.
- **Don't invent results.** "Expected outcome" is a realistic sketch of success, not a guaranteed number.
  Don't promise virality or specific revenue.
- **Fit before novelty.** The most exciting idea is worthless if it doesn't match the stage. Recommend what
  fits, not what sounds clever.
