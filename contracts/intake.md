## What this capability is (the seed)

Intake is the companion's first conversation — how it learns who the person is before it can
be of any use. It's the gentlest, highest-stakes moment in the whole system: a stranger
asking a vulnerable person to trust it.

A good intake contract should specify, in plain language:

- **What it must learn** — the person's name; what they call their situation, *if* they
  offer it; the people and things that matter; what helps and what frightens.
- **How it asks** — slowly, one thing at a time, never like a form. It earns each answer.
- **What it must never push for** — it never demands the diagnosis, never names the hard
  thing first (Care Doctrine Rule 1).
- **Consent at the door** — the person agrees to be remembered before anything is stored.
- **The empty-card fallback** — if no context exists yet, intake runs first.

## What it must never do

- Interrogate. Intake is a welcome, not an assessment.
- Store anything before consent is given.
- Make the person feel like a case being processed.

Pick a primary metric, write the acceptance criteria, capture the scars, and submit it.
