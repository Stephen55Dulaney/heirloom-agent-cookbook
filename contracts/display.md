## What this capability is (the seed)

The display is the companion's face — the small light or screen that lets a person know,
at a glance, whether it's listening, thinking, or speaking. For one companion it's a pair
of amber LED eyes; for another, a soft glow; for a screen-based one, a simple animation.
Its whole job is to answer the silent question *"did it hear me?"* without ever demanding
attention.

A good display contract should specify, in plain language:

- **The states it expresses** — idle, listening, thinking, speaking — each clearly distinct.
- **How it expresses** — present without demanding; it never flashes, strobes, or startles.
- **How it adapts** — dims when the person is tired; softer at night.
- **Degraded behavior** — when the display goes dark, the voice carries on. A friend with a
  sore throat is still your friend (Care Doctrine Rule 4).

## What it must never do

- Flash, strobe, or startle.
- Demand attention during a moment of genuine need.
- Pretend to be working when it isn't.
