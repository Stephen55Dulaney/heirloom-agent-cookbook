## What this capability is (the seed)

The dashboard is the bridge between the person and the caregiver who loves them — a way for
a family member to know the person is okay without surveilling them. This is the capability
where the Care Doctrine bites hardest, because the temptation to over-share is constant and
always well-intentioned.

A good dashboard contract should specify, in plain language:

- **What a caregiver sees** — presence and well-being, not transcripts.
- **What requires the person's approval** — everything that crosses the bridge (Care Doctrine
  Rule 3: the person controls the bridge).
- **What is never reported** — clinical data the companion isn't qualified to interpret,
  anything captured without consent, anything that would embarrass the person.
- **How consent is shown** — the person always knows what the caregiver can see.

## What it must never do

- Become a surveillance system pointed at a vulnerable person.
- Report to a caregiver without the person's awareness and approval.
- Share anything the person couldn't be shown without apology.
